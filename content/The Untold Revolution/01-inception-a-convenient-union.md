Solapur, May 18, 1945

The country, India is headed towards the end of a brutal freedom struggle and the start of a new one. The uneducated strive in their innocence, unaware of what is to come. Only one thought prevails; Freedom from the British rule. 

The struggle is not new, it had been more than four hundred years of foreign rule. And only about a ninety years ago did the country take its first steps towards theindependence movement. An atmosphere of fear with hopes of a free India run through the whims of every Indian. Many had laid down their lives fighting for complete freedom, men who would come to be known as freedom fighters had been branded terrorists and hanged for their misdemeanors. But Bhagat Singh was far from what had been propagated, he fought for what he believed in. A man of strong will who spread fear in the eyes of those he intimidated the most. A terrorist for some, a folk hero for many.

Every time there was a power struggle, the winners got to write the history. It is how the world worked.Religions and achievements have been erased from the books of history. Some we will never know. The peopleonly needed a string of hope to hang on to. And they would believe what they were told; innocent souls. They didn’t know what was coming.

Freedom was not earned despite what the common people believed. The white man had depleted all the resources from the counties it governed. They colonized many a nations, and had now reached the end of a profitable administration. It was time to go. To let the Indians bask in the remains of what was left, a land once run by monarchs, slaves had been left for the corrupt to devour.

Far away from the independence struggle, in a distant town called Solapur, the air is filled with folk songs. It is a holy union of a forty year old man who is beingremarried to a fourteen year old girl. She was too young and quick for him. And the groom’s days had driven him into a slow miserable silence. A continuous suffering. This was the age of the silent hero, none like this man.

Daud Saheb Sikander Patel had migrated to a distant town in India called Solapur, evading his brothers who had driven him away from their inherited lands. Brothers fighting brothers was not uncommon in the family. What started as a great family of landlords had soon ended in ruins. 

Daud Saheb and Mehboobi were together out of necessity. There was no emotional love between them but a marriage of societal obligations. An alliance held firm by society’s viewpoint of ‘what the world would say if they were to not continue’. 

Over the years Daud Saheb grew resentful in his marriage, the love for his first wife never fully evaded him. The only time he was truly happy. Soon, it all changed with the birth of his first child.

They had six children; three boys and three girls. The women somehow took pride in being tough, stories of women delivering babies in sugar cane fields and coming back to work the next day as if nothing had happened, were common. Mehboobi was one of them, the years had made her tough. She was not one to easily back down, a steadfast woman.

Mehboobi spent her days working hard. She would not let go of any opportunity she could find. A shrewdwoman who was not going to let the world walk over her. She didn’t wait for her husband to come around, while he went off to the mills to earn the day’s paycheck, she would take care of her children and find ways to get ahead. Knit, barter, negotiate, fight, she was ready for what came her way. Never backing down, never waiting for her husband’s approval.

They grew distant over the years. Their wavelengths years apart, they could not come to common terms. A mystic and a capitalist, they did what they had to.
