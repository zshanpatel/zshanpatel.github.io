June 1, 1956

The cries of a baby boy echoed through the maternity ward. It was the first time Mehboobi delivered a baby in a hospital. It made no difference to her; the hospital bed or the fields. 

“The bills” she thought, “the fields would have been cheaper and much more convenient.” She yelled out in a condescending tone. The hospital made it mandatory for her to rest. She wouldn’t have it. She was notorious when it came to enduring. ‘Who was going to take care of business in my absence” she thought. 

Daud Saheb named his third child Ishaque; the smiling one. 

The boy rarely cried, not aware of the life that lay ahead of him, he was a happy child. He spent most of his days doing what was told, only revolting silently when he didn’t have his way.

This was not an easy place, it was a dreadful one in fact. Lies and disease was common. People worked hard to make ends meet. Brute force was used commonly to get what one wanted. Often the family had to be careful of not getting caught up in the delinquencies of their neighbors. Mehboobi was one of the few who cared about giving her children an education, even though she preferred the boys over the girls.

Years had passed.

Now ten years old, a dainty little boy, Ishaque would stay close to his mother. Helping her where he could. He was her most trusted child. Every day after school, he would come back to her as quickly as he could as though a shadow catching up to its owner. And Mehboobi would send him away to work at the railway station, sometimes selling tea, sometimes ice cream. He didn’t mind. He was happy to bring back what he could earn and support his family in whatever way he could. Tiring as it was he would always come back with a smile on his face. A happy little momma’s boy. 

One day he returned late in the evening to find that his sister spread like a log at the spot where he slept.

“What happened Ishaque?” his sister asked him while lazily laying in the corner. It was a small room.

“Nothing.” Ishaque murmured under his breath. 

His mother soon catching up to his distress. The whole family had to sleep in one cozy room, next to each other. Sense of privacy was unknown.

“Get off his spot!” Mehboobi yelled out. Saberasuddenly shuddering to her mother’s roar, jolted straight up on her feet without realizing what was going on.

“Okay Amma, I’m going, I’m going” she had finally caught up to Ishaque’s throughts. She was in his spot and he was going to stand there not bothering his little sister.
