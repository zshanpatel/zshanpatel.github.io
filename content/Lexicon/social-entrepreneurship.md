> Social entrepreneurship is the application of business models to social problems, converting need into market opportunity while leaving root causes intact.

Social entrepreneurship presents itself as the humane face of capitalism: markets that solve problems rather than create them. The social entrepreneur identifies a need, designs a product or service, and generates revenue by meeting it. Hunger becomes a business model. Poverty becomes a market. Education becomes a product.

The problem is not that social entrepreneurs lack good intentions. The problem is that the market mechanism itself cannot address structural causes. Selling sugar to children while calling it nutrition education does not solve hunger. Charging for clean water does not address the infrastructure that made it scarce. The social enterprise operates within the same economic hierarchy that produced the problem, and its success depends on that hierarchy remaining intact.

Social entrepreneurship serves as ideology by offering individual action as substitute for systemic change. If the right business model can solve poverty, then poverty is a design problem rather than a political one. This relieves pressure on the structures that concentrate wealth and distribute opportunity unequally.
