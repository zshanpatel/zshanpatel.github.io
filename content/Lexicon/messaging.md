> Messaging is the controlled translation of what an organisation stands for into the claims an audience is meant to remember and believe.

Communication becomes incoherent when every person, channel, and campaign invents its own explanation of what an organisation offers. More content does not solve that problem. Without a shared hierarchy of claims, attention is spent repeating disconnected features, tones, and promises.

Messaging turns a positioning into a usable communication system. It identifies the primary idea, the message pillars that support it, the proof points that make those pillars credible, and the action the audience is being asked to take. The framework gives different teams a common answer to what should be said, to whom, and why it should matter.

Messaging is not the same as a slogan or a single piece of copy. A slogan is one expression of the system. Messaging is the underlying set of ideas that can be adapted across advertising, sales, social media, presentations, and product communication while preserving the same strategic meaning. Good messaging is concise, relevant, differentiated, tailored to its audience, and supported by reasons to believe.

*Source: Lumen Learning, "Defining the Message," Principles of Marketing. Open educational source defining messaging as key points that translate positioning into communication for a target audience.*
