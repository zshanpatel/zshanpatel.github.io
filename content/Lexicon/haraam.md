> Haraam is not simply "something I dislike"; it is a verdict that stands under divine authority and is therefore morally, legally, and spiritually binding.

In a machine, formal similarity collapses the difference between *[[haraam|(haraam)]]* and ordinary immorality. But the difference is not lexical; it is ontological. Haraam is part of a lawful order in which God defines what is forbidden, and humans are not free to erase that boundary by changing their preferences. This is why the concept is not just a negative mood. It is an objective standard, a juridical prohibition, and a moral signal that a thing damages the soul, the community, or the covenantal order.

The problem with modern AI is not that it fails to "understand the word." It fails to understand the source of authority underneath the word. It sees a social category, a negative label, or an avoided outcome. It does not see the command embedded in revelation, nor the legal and spiritual structure that makes the prohibition what it is. The machine reduces a divine prohibition to a preference-shaped avoidance heuristic.
