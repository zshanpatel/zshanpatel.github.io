> The periphery is the structural position occupied by those excluded from centres of power, the margin from which hungrier groups historically rise to overthrow comfortable centres.

In Ibn Khaldun's civilisational theory, the periphery is where group solidarity remains strong. Those at the margins of power, excluded from the comfort and spoils of the centre, retain the cohesion and hunger that the centre has lost. The periphery is not merely a geographic location but a structural position: those who depend on collective action for survival, who have not been softened by sedentary comfort, who retain the capacity for sacrifice and coordination.

The periphery challenges the centre not because of ideology but because of structure. The centre has lost its solidarity. The periphery retains it. The centre is comfortable. The periphery is hungry. The law of the jungle dictates that the hungry consume the comfortable.

The essay positions Muslim brown men at the periphery of contemporary power structures: excluded from centres of economic, political, and cultural authority, yet retaining the capacity for collective action that those centres have lost. The periphery is both a condition of exclusion and a position of potential transformation.
