> Build for descendants you will never meet, and hold what you build as a trust rather than a trophy.

Short-term systems reward extraction because their accounting horizon ends at the next result. A family, institution, or civilisation can therefore consume the conditions of its own continuity while still reporting success. Long-horizon thinking restores the people who are missing from that calculation: those who come after us.

Seven-generation legacy is used here as a stewardship heuristic. It asks whether a decision leaves future descendants with greater agency, stronger relationships, sounder institutions, and real productive assets, rather than merely transferring money or status. The seven-generation number supplies discipline and scale. It is not a prediction, a financial product, or a guarantee that a family will remain intact.

The wider seven-generation stewardship idea is commonly associated with Haudenosaunee teaching, but the exact popular formulation and its attribution are unevenly documented online. This Lexicon entry uses seven-generation legacy as an authorial framework, not as a claim that this exact phrase is an established academic term. Its Islamic register is stewardship under trust, where wealth and authority are held for obligations that extend beyond the present owner.

The test is simple: would this choice still look responsible if its costs arrived after our names were forgotten? That moves legacy away from vanity and toward durable capacity, including the capacity to pursue an [[afterlife-orientation|afterlife orientation]] while building in the [[dunya|dunya]].

Source note: no adequate academic source for the exact phrase “seven-generation legacy” was verified. Web research found the related “seven generation stewardship” formulation, but the accessible reference itself flags citation gaps. The attribution is therefore retained cautiously and not presented as settled scholarship.
