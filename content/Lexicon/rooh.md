> Freud's superego restricts. The rooh, in this reading, liberates — same structural role in the model, opposite verdict on what that role is for.

In the same structured Islamic-psychology comparison that maps *[[Lexicon/nafs|nafs]]* onto Freud's id and *[[Lexicon/qalb|qalb]]* onto Freud's ego, the rooh occupies the position Freud gave the superego: the internalised authority the rest of the psyche answers to. Freudian theory treats the superego as structurally restrictive by nature, a set of internalised social prohibitions pressing down on the self. This reading inverts that verdict. Because the rooh's authority is divine rather than merely social, it "transcends dimensional limits, extending beyond societal or material constraints" — its function isn't to constrain the self on behalf of a given society's norms, but to orient it toward a reality that outlives and outranks any particular society's norms. Liberation, on this account, comes from the qalb and rooh working in harmony rather than the qalb operating alone or against the rooh's pull.
