> The self-improvement industrial complex sells an answer to the feeling that you are never enough.

A culture that treats the self as permanently improvable creates a durable market. If dissatisfaction is framed as a personal deficiency, each new failure can become a reason to buy another method, subscription, course, assessment, or coach. The market does not need improvement to end. It needs the customer to remain an unfinished project.

The self-improvement industrial complex is the commercial system built around that demand. It includes the publishing, coaching, training, wellness, productivity, therapeutic, fitness, and digital self-tracking markets that package personal change as products and services. Its defining feature is not that every product is useless. It is that the desire for development becomes organised as repeat consumption, with success measured through engagement, conversion, visibility, or continued purchase.

This system should be distinguished from personal development itself. People can learn, discipline themselves, seek counsel, and cultivate character without entering a market. It should also be distinguished from self-optimisation, which is the psychological strategy of managing the self as a project. The industrial complex supplies the surrounding infrastructure, incentives, and commodities that make that strategy profitable.

*Source: Cabanas, Edgar, and Eva Illouz. Manufacturing Happy Citizens: How the Science and Industry of Happiness Control Our Lives. Polity, 2019.
