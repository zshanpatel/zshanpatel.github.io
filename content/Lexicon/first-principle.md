> A foundational truth that cannot be reduced further — the bedrock from which all valid reasoning, design, and action must ultimately derive.

Most human thinking is not first-principle thinking. It is analogical: we copy what has worked before, iterate on existing templates, and inherit assumptions we have never tested. This produces incremental improvements to broken systems rather than genuinely new ones.

First-principle reasoning requires stripping a problem down to its most basic, verifiable truths and rebuilding from there. The approach is credited in modern discourse to Aristotle and popularised by physicists and engineers — but its deepest expression is theological. The monotheistic tradition begins with the most radical first principle of all: that reality has a single, unified source of truth *[[tawhid|(tawhid)]]*, and that all valid knowledge must ultimately trace back to it.

This has practical consequences:
- In **epistemology**: knowledge must be grounded in revelation and verified reason, not inherited secular consensus *[[intrinsic-epistemic|(intrinsic epistemology)]]*.
- In **economics**: value must trace back to real assets and ethical exchange, not debt creation *[[riba|(riba)]]*.
- In **identity**: the self must be grounded in its created nature *[[fitra|(fitra)]]*, not in social performance or market utility.

Building from first principles is the antidote to the NPC state *[[npc|(NPC)]]* — it requires the courage to discard comfortable assumptions and the discipline to reason from what is actually true.
