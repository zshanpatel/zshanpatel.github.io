> The modern education system does not fail by accident. It produces exactly what it was designed to produce: compliant, credentialled workers with no memory of what they were separated from.

The secular education system is the institutional apparatus through which children are removed from parental care for the majority of their formative years and placed under state-directed instruction. It presents itself as neutral, universal, and in the child's interest. It is none of these things. It is a specific historical invention with specific origins, specific interests, and specific consequences that its own framework cannot evaluate because those consequences are its product.

John Bowlby's attachment theory established that the quality of a child's bond with primary caregivers is the foundation of emotional regulation, self-concept, and the capacity for meaningful relationships throughout life. Secure attachment, formed through consistent, responsive parental presence in the earliest years, produces psychological resilience. Insecure attachment, formed through prolonged separation or inconsistent caregiving, produces anxiety, avoidance, or disorganised patterns that persist into adulthood. The research is unambiguous on this point.

The secular education system interrupts this process. It takes children from the age of four or five and places them in institutional environments staffed by strangers, governed by compliance schedules, and oriented toward standardised academic outcomes. The child's primary attachment figures, the people biologically and spiritually programmed toward that child's best interests, are replaced by rotating adults operating within bureaucratic constraints. The message, however unintentional, is clear: the family is insufficient. The institution knows better. The child belongs to the system.

What follows is not education in any classical sense. The trivium and quadrivium trained the mind toward wisdom. The modern curriculum trains the child toward economic utility. Subjects are fragmented into specialisations. Learning is divorced from lived experience. Spiritual formation is excluded by design, not by oversight. The secular framework treats the child as a future worker and the school as the processing facility. This is the education-industrial-complex: an apparatus that produces predictable, credentialled outputs while systematically neglecting the spiritual, moral, and emotional development that would make those outputs actually human.

The psychological cost is measurable. Parent-child separation in childhood, even the routine separation of institutional schooling, produces documented effects on social-emotional development and wellbeing. Children raised primarily by institutions and screens rather than by parents develop different attachment patterns, different capacity for sustained attention, and different relationships with authority. They do not become more independent. They become more dependent, on peer validation, on algorithmic feedback, on whatever substitute the system offers for the parental bond it displaced.
