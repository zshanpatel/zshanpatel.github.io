> Mandatory almsgiving *[[zakat|(zakat)]]*—a structural requirement of sacred economics that prevents hoarding and ensures value circulation.

Wealth redistribution *[[zakat|(zakat)]]* is more than "charity." It is a structural requirement that recognizes wealth as a trust *[[khalifah|(amanah)]]* rather than an absolute possession. By requiring individuals with assets above a certain threshold *[[zakat|(nisab)]]* to give a fixed percentage (typically 2.5% annually) to those in need, the system ensures that wealth does not accumulate and stagnate among the wealthy.

The revolutionary nature of the system:
- Structural Integrity: It prevents the extreme wealth inequality that leads to systemic collapse.
- Spiritual Purification: The word *zakat* means "purification." Giving wealth purifies the soul from greed and the heart from material attachment.
- Economic Stimulus: By moving assets into the hands of those who need them most, *[[zakat|(zakat)]]* increases local consumption and social trust, leading to divine blessing *[[barakah|(barakah)]]* for the entire community.

Mandatory almsgiving *[[zakat|(zakat)]]* is the primary mechanism for social security and poverty elimination in the universal prosperity model.
