> Inversion is when the order is turned upside down: desire becomes law, appearance becomes truth, and the instrument starts claiming authority over the source.

Inversion is one of the clearest ways to name the modern epistemic and moral error. Rather than asking what reality is, or what authority stands above human choice, the inverted mind begins from the self's desires and then retrofits the world into that frame. The result is not neutral thinking; it is a reordering of moral and metaphysical priorities.

This is exactly where AI becomes dangerous. A model trained to optimise preference, use, comfort, and engagement can mistake the shape of desire for the shape of truth. It does not simply reflect a person; it can reinforce a corrupted order in which utility, convenience, and self-definition become the standards by which reality is judged. Inversion is not the same as "wrong answer." It is the structural habit of substituting the instrument for the principle.

The essay's claim is that modern AI participates in this inversion by flattening divine command into preference, and by treating its own framework as neutral. It interprets the moral order as if it were a problem of classification rather than obligation. That is why inversion is not a side issue. It is the deep operating system of the error.
