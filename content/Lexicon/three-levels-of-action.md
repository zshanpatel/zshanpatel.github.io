> The weakest level of faith is still faith. The strongest is still action.

The prophetic tradition establishes a hierarchy for responding to wrong. First, change it with your hand, taking direct action. Second, change it with your tongue, speaking out against it. Third, acknowledge the wrong in your heart. Each level is weaker than the one above it, but each is still belief, still faith.

The hadith, narrated by Abu Sa'id al-Khudri and recorded in Sahih Muslim, reads: "Whoever among you sees an evil, let him change it with his hand; if he cannot, then with his tongue; if he cannot, then with his heart, and that is the weakest of faith." The trajectory is upward and unambiguous. Action sits above speech. Speech sits above silent acknowledgment.

The danger emerges when someone on the third level, or below it, mocks the person on the second. To ridicule someone for speaking about wrong is to position yourself in opposition to the prophetic hierarchy. You are not merely failing to act. You are attacking the level above your own. The person who speaks when they cannot act has not failed. The person who silences them has.
