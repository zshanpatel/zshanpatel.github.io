> Not decoration applied after the message is decided — the study of how the visual and verbal choices themselves are the message.

Communication design sits at the intersection of graphic design and rhetoric: it treats typography, colour, layout, and imagery as carrying meaning in their own right, not as packaging wrapped around a separately-decided message. The academic grounding for this is social semiotics — Gunther Kress's multimodality theory treats images, layout, and text as parallel "modes" that jointly construct meaning, rather than treating writing as primary and visuals as secondary illustration.

There isn't a single peer-reviewed paper that defines "communication design" as a named discipline the way there is for design thinking — the citation here is to the semiotic theory that underpins how the field actually reasons about meaning, a genuine but adjacent source, worth being explicit about.
