> Technocratic governance is political organisation where technical expertise and measurable metrics replace public judgment and moral reasoning as the basis for collective decisions.

Technocratic governance extends the logic of technocracy into the full structure of political life. Expertise becomes the primary source of political authority. Measurement becomes the primary language of policy. Efficiency becomes the primary criterion of success. Public judgment, moral reasoning, and democratic participation are treated as obstacles to good governance rather than its foundation.

The problem is not that expertise is irrelevant to governance. Complex systems require technical knowledge. The problem is that expertise answers questions of means without settling questions of ends. A model can optimise growth, stability, or efficiency, but it cannot establish whether those should outrank dignity, freedom, justice, or divine obligation. When expertise replaces judgment, the selection of ends becomes invisible: the experts choose what to optimise for, and that choice is itself political, not technical.

Technocratic governance functions as a mechanism for removing political choices from democratic contestation. If a policy is what the data requires, disagreement becomes ignorance rather than legitimate difference. If a target is what the model demands, alternatives become impossible rather than merely unchosen.
