> Comfort is settled material security that dissolves the group solidarity forged through hardship, structurally preconditioning civilisational decline.

Comfort is the structural opposite of the conditions that produce group solidarity. When survival is assured, the incentive for collective action diminishes. When material needs are met, the urgency that produced sacrifice and coordination fades. When individual pursuit becomes possible, collective obligation weakens.

The essay describes comfort as a phase in the civilisational cycle: "You get rich, you get fat, you get lazy." This is not a moral judgment about individual behaviour but a structural observation about what happens when the conditions that produce solidarity are eliminated by the success solidarity produced.

Comfort is dangerous not because it is pleasant but because it is unstable. The comfortable lose the capacity for collective action. The hungry retain it. The result is predictable: the comfortable are consumed by the desperate. Comfort is not a permanent achievement but a temporary condition that precedes its own destruction.
