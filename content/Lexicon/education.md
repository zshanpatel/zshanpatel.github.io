> Education either produces conformity or it produces freedom. Freire argued there is no third option.

Ask what education is for and most answers describe method: better teachers, better tests, better content. Paulo Freire's *Pedagogy of the Oppressed* asks a more structural question, and answers it with a binary rather than a spectrum.

"Banking" education treats the student as an empty account into which a teacher deposits facts, detached from the reality that gave them meaning. Freire argued this model assumes a split between people and the world, the student is a spectator, not a participant, and that a docile, non-questioning student body is exactly what keeps existing systems of power comfortable. The alternative, problem-posing education, is dialogical: teacher and student question reality together, dissolving the strict hierarchy between them into "teacher-students" and "student-teachers."

Freire's own framing is unambiguous: education either functions to integrate people into the present system and produce conformity, or it becomes "the practice of freedom, the means by which men and women deal critically and creatively with reality and discover how to participate in the transformation of their world." Its goal is *conscientização*, critical consciousness, the capacity to see the contradictions in one's own conditions and act on them, not to accumulate credentials.

*Source: Paulo Freire, Pedagogy of the Oppressed (1970).*
