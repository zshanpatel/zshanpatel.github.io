> Not information accumulated for its own sake, but understanding pursued as a form of worship — the world, the self, and the divine, held as one connected inquiry.

Modern education treats knowledge as a means to an end: a credential, a skill set, a salary. *'Ilm* inverts that hierarchy. In the Islamic intellectual tradition, seeking knowledge is itself a religious obligation, not a means to one — early scholars in Baghdad, Cordoba, and Cairo studied mathematics, astronomy, philosophy, and jurisprudence as facets of a single pursuit, not as separate, monetisable disciplines competing for a student's time.

This is also why the tradition never separated "useful" fields from "useless" ones the way modern vocational framing does. Rational inquiry and revelation were treated as partners, not rivals — a structural reason the Islamic Golden Age's scholars moved fluidly between astronomy, poetry, and theology in ways a modern single-major degree structure makes difficult to imagine.
