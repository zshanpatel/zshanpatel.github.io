> The feminine is circular: contained, designed for offspring resource-gathering. The masculine is linear and forward-moving. The man seeks martyrdom. Death for a noble cause.

The confusion between these two orientations produces the central dysfunction of gynocentric social systems. When circular patterns are treated as the universal default, linear motion is pathologised as aggression. When containment is elevated over direction, the man who moves toward a cause is diagnosed as reckless rather than purposeful.

Robert Trivers' parental investment theory (1972) provides the evolutionary grounding: the sex investing more per offspring (typically female) develops strategies oriented toward resource-gathering and protection within a contained space, while the sex investing less (typically male) develops competitive, outward-directed strategies. Carol Gilligan's In a Different Voice (1982) extended this into moral psychology, distinguishing the "ethic of care," relational and contextual, from the "ethic of justice," principled and universal. The feminine operates within a circle. The masculine operates along a line.

The circular pattern is not a deficiency. It is a design: a secure concentration of emotion directed inward and contained, biologically oriented toward gathering resources for offspring. The masculine pattern is also a design: forward motion through risk and sacrifice, seeking something above personal comfort. The fem-man inverts this. He retreats into softness as a cause. The martyrdom is replaced by things. The sacrifice is replaced by performance.
