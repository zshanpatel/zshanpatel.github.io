> Rebranding is the alteration of an entity's public identity to access new markets, legitimacy, or tolerance while leaving its fundamental operations unchanged.

Rebranding operates as a surface transformation that preserves structural continuity. The colonial power rebrands as development agency. The extractive corporation rebrands as sustainability partner. The oppressive system rebrands as reformed institution. The name changes, the narrative changes, the visual identity changes. The underlying distribution of power and resources remains intact.

The effectiveness of rebranding depends on the willingness of audiences to accept surface change as substantive change. This willingness is itself structured: those who benefit from the existing arrangement have incentive to accept rebranding as sufficient, while those who suffer from it recognise the continuity beneath the surface.

In the context of global power, rebranding allows historical extraction to continue under new management. The language of partnership replaces the language of domination. The rhetoric of shared value replaces the rhetoric of exploitation. The structural relationship remains unchanged while its public expression is renovated for contemporary sensibilities.
