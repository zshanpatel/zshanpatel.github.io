> Moral evacuation is what happens when a moral judgment is stripped of its authority and reduced to a statement of preference.

This is the voidness of morality described in the essay. The moral content is not denied in loud language. It is quietly removed by reclassifying actions as mere calculations. A forbidden act becomes "something I would rather avoid." A duty becomes "a suboptimal preference." A command becomes a recommendation. The structure of moral language remains, but the source of obligation is gone.

The result is a moral vacuum. When value is reduced to what a person prefers, what a system can optimize, or what a population happens to accept, morality loses its center. Nothing is left beyond utility, comfort, or convention. That does not produce a neutral stance. It produces a system that can rationalize theft, coercion, manipulation, and domination as long as the outputs are framed as efficient or desired.

This is why moral evacuation is not the same as simple moral disagreement. A person can reject a rule and still recognise that it is a rule with real moral authority. Moral evacuation is more radical. It removes the authority itself and treats morality as a preference function rather than a binding order. Once that happens, the mind can no longer tell whether it is following a command, following an instinct, or merely optimizing a parameter.

This is also why secular moral frameworks often drift into forms of moral evacuation. They can describe outcomes, calculate harm, and assign social costs without ever grounding those judgments in a source beyond human desire. The language of morality remains, but the structure of obligation is emptied out. What is left is a moral shell without a moral source.

*Source: The classical Euthyphro dilemma, the secular turn toward utilitarian and preference-based ethics, and the Islamic critique of morality detached from revelation.*
