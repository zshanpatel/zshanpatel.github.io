> Ego-worship is not merely liking yourself. It is treating the self as the final authority to which truth, desire, and action must answer.

Modern self-cultures often present self-optimisation as neutral improvement. The danger begins when every improvement is judged by what it adds to the person's status, pleasure, control, or image, while no higher measure is allowed to question the project. The self is no longer being developed for a purpose. It becomes the purpose.

Ego-worship is therefore a form of self-referential elevation. It can appear as vanity, but it does not require visible arrogance. A person can worship the ego through private resentment, compulsive achievement, the pursuit of admiration, or the belief that personal preference is sufficient evidence of what is right. The common structure is that the self's appetite or narrative is treated as sovereign.

This distinguishes ego-worship from healthy self-respect and from disciplined self-development. A self can be strengthened, educated, and cared for without being made ultimate. In a God-centred account, the self is a trust and an instrument of action, not the source of its own final meaning. Its desires require examination and training, not automatic obedience. That is the domain of [[nafs|nafs]] and the discipline of reorientation toward [[divine-purpose|divine purpose]].

The distinction matters because a person who serves the ego can mistake expansion for growth. More money, beauty, knowledge, influence, or freedom may enlarge the self's reach while leaving its direction unchanged. What appears to be liberation can become deeper dependence on recognition, consumption, and comparison.

*Source: Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Narcissus," on narcissism as inflated self-image or excessive self-involvement.*
