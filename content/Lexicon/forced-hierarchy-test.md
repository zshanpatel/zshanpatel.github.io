> The practice that forces you to abandon what felt most important, five times a day, and stand bare before God.

Modern life is a constant negotiation of priorities, each desire claiming urgency, each commitment demanding precedence. The problem is not that people lack priorities. It is that they never test them. The things that feel most important go unchallenged because nothing interrupts them at their peak.

Prayer, practiced five times daily on time, functions as a forced hierarchy test. At the moment a desire is strongest, at the peak of a plan or an attachment, the practitioner stops. People had to be overcome. Tasks had to be abandoned. The ego had to be set aside. What remains when you are standing, bowing, bending, with your forehead on the ground? What did you have to give up to be present here?

The test is not intellectual. It is structural. The physical act of submission, the timing that interrupts at peak desire, the bareness of the posture. These force a confrontation with the real hierarchy of the self, not the one declared in words but the one lived in action. Five times a day, the question is asked again, and the answer changes as the ego resubmits.
