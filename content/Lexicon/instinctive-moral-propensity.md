> The pre-rational, cross-civilisational human capacity to sense moral truth — an innate faculty that precedes ideology, culture, and formal religion.

Secular materialism insists that morality is constructed — a social agreement, a product of evolution, a cultural convention. If this were true, we would expect moral intuitions to diverge entirely across isolated civilisations. Instead, we find the opposite: prohibitions on murder, theft, betrayal, and injustice appear independently across cultures that had no contact with one another.

This convergence is not coincidence. It is evidence of an innate moral faculty — what the Islamic tradition calls the *fitra* *[[fitra|(fitra)]]*, and what philosophers across traditions have recognised as natural law, moral intuition, or conscience.

Instinctive moral propensity is not infallible. It can be suppressed, distorted, or overridden by systematic conditioning, pathological deviation *[[pathological-deviation|(pathological deviation)]]*, and compound ignorance *[[compound-ignorance|(compound ignorance)]]*. But it cannot be entirely extinguished. Its persistence across history and civilisation is one of the strongest arguments for a transcendent, universal moral source.

Understanding this faculty matters for how we build systems. Any institution — economic, legal, educational — that operates against instinctive moral propensity will eventually generate resistance, dysfunction, or collapse. Aligning with it, rather than suppressing it, is the foundation of durable civilisational design.
