> Hunger is the condition of lacking necessities, functioning as both a physical state and the structural driver that forges group solidarity and powers civilisational change.

In Ibn Khaldun's analysis, hunger is not merely a biological state but a social condition that produces specific capacities. Those who are hungry develop the collective cohesion, sacrifice, and coordination necessary for survival. This cohesion is the foundation of political power: the group that can act collectively, sacrifice individual interest for group survival, and coordinate effectively under pressure is the group that rises.

The essay distinguishes between material hunger and structural hunger. Material hunger is the absence of food. Structural hunger is the absence of access, opportunity, and recognition. Both produce the same effect: they forge the solidarity that enables collective action. The hungry are dangerous not because they are violent but because they are cohesive.

The cycle predicts that hunger is temporary in its current form. The comfortable become complacent. The hungry become organised. The hungry consume the comfortable. The cycle resets. Hunger is not a permanent condition but a recurring one, a structural feature of systems that concentrate resources without distributing justice.
