> Divine moral supremacy is the Islamic position that God's revelation provides the highest and most complete standard for moral, social, and political organisation, above which no human system can legitimately place itself.

The claim of divine moral supremacy is not a claim about personal piety. It is a structural claim about the source of legitimate authority. If moral standards derive from human reason, cultural convention, or political consensus, then those with power define what counts as moral. If moral standards derive from divine revelation, then power is subject to standards it did not create and cannot revise.

This distinction matters because it determines whether existing systems can be fundamentally questioned. A technocracy that defines its own metrics of success cannot be challenged by standards it does not recognise. An economic hierarchy that defines its own terms of fairness cannot be judged by principles it does not acknowledge. Divine moral supremacy provides the external standpoint from which these systems can be evaluated and found wanting.

The suppression of this claim is itself a form of epistemic control. When the highest standard of moral authority is removed from public discourse, the only remaining standards are those produced by the systems that benefit from not being judged. The result is a moral vacuum in which power defines justice rather than being subject to it.
