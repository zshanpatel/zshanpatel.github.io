> A patriarchal system is where men lead in the shadow of God, not wasting energy on trivialities. Bravery is rewarded. Risk is managed. The divine system.

The term "patriarchy" has been claimed by multiple traditions with opposing evaluations. In feminist political theory, it denotes a system of social, political, and economic structures in which men as a group maintain power over women. Kate Millett's Sexual Politics (1969) used the term to describe male domination and the power relationships by which men control women. Nancy Folbre's The Rise and Decline of Patriarchal Systems (1993) conceptualised patriarchal systems as structures of collective power delivering exploitative advantages based on gender, class, and other group memberships.

This entry reclaims the term from that framing. The patriarchal system, as used here, is the divine system: a social order in which men lead under divine accountability, directing energy toward building consistency and strength rather than spending it on trivialities. There are no emotional pendulum swings. Bravery is rewarded and risk is managed, with high tolerance of it revered as a virtue.

The distinction is structural. The feminist critique describes patriarchy as oppression. This entry describes it as orientation: men leading in service of something above themselves, maintaining the frame through which collective purpose is held. The divine system does not oscillate. It builds.
