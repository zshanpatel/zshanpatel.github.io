> Social impact is the measurable effect of interventions on social conditions, serving as both evaluation tool and ideology that individualises structural problems.

The language of social impact converts systemic problems into measurable outcomes. Poverty becomes a metric to be reduced. Inequality becomes a programme to be evaluated. The question shifts from "why does this condition exist?" to "what intervention produced what measurable change?" This shift is not neutral. It directs attention toward intervention and away from causation.

Social impact measurement serves the existing order by making structural problems appear amenable to programme-level solutions. If impact can be measured, it can be managed. If it can be managed, it does not require transformation. The question becomes how to optimise interventions within existing structures, not whether those structures produce the conditions being addressed.

The concept also functions as legitimacy. Organisations that demonstrate social impact justify their existence, their funding, and their operating space. The demand for impact measurement is itself a form of control: it requires organisations to translate their work into the language of measurable outcomes, which is the language of the systems that produced the problems in the first place.
