> Anxiety-driven consumption buys relief from an unsettled inner state, not simply a useful object.

Anxious consumption begins when uncertainty or perceived inadequacy becomes a market opportunity. A product, course, supplement, or status signal is presented as the missing solution. Buying creates a brief sense of movement and control, even when the purchase does not address the source of the anxiety.

The cycle is self-reinforcing. Advertising identifies a fear, attaches that fear to an unmet desire, and offers consumption as the next action. Relief fades, the original uncertainty returns, and another purchase becomes attractive. The market does not need to resolve the anxiety if it can keep converting anxiety into demand.

This concept does not claim that every purchase is pathological. It identifies a specific pattern in which consumption functions primarily as emotional regulation and temporary reassurance.

Source: Bauman, *Consuming Life*; Dittmar, *Consumer Culture, Identity, and Well-Being*.
