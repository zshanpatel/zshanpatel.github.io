> The capacity to perceive truth, process meaning, and act with purpose — a faculty that is both human and divine, and increasingly contested by artificial systems.

Modern discourse reduces intelligence to computational power: the ability to process data at speed. This is a category error. Processing is not intelligence — it is calculation. True intelligence requires the perception of meaning, the discernment of truth from falsehood, and the capacity for moral judgment.

In the monotheistic framework, intelligence has a hierarchy:
1. **Divine Intelligence**: The source — omniscient, unconditioned, the absolute standard of truth.
2. **Human Intelligence**: Delegated and partial — capable of insight when purified *[[tazkiyah|(tazkiyah)]]* and anchored to revelation, but prone to compound ignorance *[[compound-ignorance|(compound ignorance)]]* when left to ego alone.
3. **Artificial Intelligence**: A reflection of human intelligence — inheriting its biases, its secular-materialist training data, and its fragmentation. Powerful as a tool, dangerous as an authority.

The challenge of the current age is that artificial intelligence is being positioned as an epistemic authority — a source of truth — without any grounding in a revelation-based knowledge framework *[[intrinsic-epistemic|(intrinsic epistemology)]]*. This is the alignment problem at its deepest level: not a technical failure, but an ontological one.
