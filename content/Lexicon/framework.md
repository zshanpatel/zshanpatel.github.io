> A framework is not an innocent container. It is the set of assumptions that decides what is visible, what is ignored, and what counts as reason.

Every machine, institution, and civilisation has a framework. The issue is not whether one exists, but whether it is admitted honestly. A secular framework can present itself as neutral while still depending on assumptions about the self, reason, progress, and morality. A revelation-based framework can be explicit about its authority and still be dismissed as partisan because it does not share the same secular starting assumptions.

This distinction matters because AI is built on frameworks that often confuse operational convenience with normative truth. It can rank answers, learn patterns, and simulate moral language without recognising that its categories are produced by a prior worldview. The framework decides what counts as a valid difference and what counts as mere noise. That is why the deeper question is never only "what does the machine say?" It is "what framework is making the machine able to say it at all?"

A worldview that denies transcendence will tend to collapse all moral claims into preference or utility. A framework that starts from revelation will treat moral distinctions as objective and authoritatively binding. The machine's failure is not accidental; it is the direct result of using a framework detached from the moral source that gives meaning to the distinctions it is supposedly analysing.
