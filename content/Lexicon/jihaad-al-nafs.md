> *Jihaad* translates loosely to "strive or struggle," a struggle Islamic thought treats as both external and internal.

*Jihaad al-nafs* is the internal application: the deliberate, ongoing effort to challenge the ego's dominance and the individualist thinking that follows from letting the *[[Lexicon/nafs|nafs]]* run unchecked. Left to itself, an unrestrained *[[Lexicon/qalb|qalb]]* (the ego-like faculty managing desire) produces the pattern this vault elsewhere calls financial neurosis: material dependence, indulgence in worldly desire, and hyper-consumption dressed up as personal freedom.

This is the struggle, not the outcome it aims at. *[[Lexicon/tazkiyah|Tazkiyat al-nafs]]* (purification of the self) is the resulting harmony, where the *qalb* and *[[Lexicon/rooh|rooh]]* work together toward divine enlightenment rather than against each other. Conflating the two loses something real: jihaad al-nafs names the ongoing effort and resistance, tazkiyah names the cleansing state that effort is meant to produce. One is the fight; the other is what winning it looks like.
