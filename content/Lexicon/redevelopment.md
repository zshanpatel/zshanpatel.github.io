> Redevelopment is not becoming a better version of the existing self. It is rebuilding the self around a different end.

Self-improvement usually assumes that the self is the project and that progress means adding capability, status, discipline, wealth, or attractiveness to it. That assumption can turn growth into a more efficient form of ego worship. The person becomes a portfolio of upgrades, measured by outcomes that remain inside the same material horizon.

Redevelopment names a different movement. It begins by questioning the end toward which development is directed. The task is not to optimise the existing identity but to expose its inherited desires, remove what is misaligned, and rebuild action around divine purpose, accountability, and service. Physical strength, mental discipline, and financial competence still matter, but they become instruments rather than objects of worship.

The term is best treated as an authorial coinage, not an established technical term in psychology, philosophy, or the personal-development industry. Its value is conceptual and contrastive. It gives a name to a form of inner reconstruction that ordinary self-improvement language tends to flatten into performance, productivity, and personal advantage.

Redevelopment is therefore closer to [[tazkiyah|Tazkiyah]] than to a motivational upgrade, though the terms are not identical. Tazkiyah names systematic purification of the self. Redevelopment extends that movement into a wider reorganisation of the body, mind, finances, and practical direction. It also confronts the [[nafs|Nafs]] without treating the self as an enemy to erase, and recovers [[fitra|Fitra]] as an original orientation toward truth and the divine.
