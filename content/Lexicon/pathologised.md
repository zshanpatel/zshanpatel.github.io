> When strength becomes a symptom, the diagnosis is the disease.

Pathologisation is the process of taking behaviour that was once understood as natural, functional, or morally meaningful, and redefining it as a disorder, a dysfunction, or a social harm. The mechanism is simple: relabel the trait, and you gain the authority to treat it. What was once courage becomes aggression. What was once leadership becomes narcissism. What was once discipline becomes rigidity.

The pattern is most visible in the treatment of [[masculine-leadership|masculine traits]]. Boys who are restless are medicated. Men who are assertive are flagged. Fathers who lead their families are reframed as controlling. The entire spectrum of masculine behaviour has been recoded as a spectrum of dysfunction, each point on it requiring intervention, therapy, or re-education.

This is not a clinical observation. It is a political strategy. When you pathologise a trait, you delegitimise the people who carry it. You create a population that second-guesses its own instincts. You produce men who apologise for being men. The culture has pathologised masculinity so thoroughly that many men now experience their own natural impulses as evidence of personal failure rather than evidence of a functioning design.

In Islamic psychology, the concept is inverted. The *[[nafs]]* is not pathologised. It is named, understood, and worked on through *[[jihaad-al-nafs]]*. The ego is not a disorder. It is a challenge. The distinction matters: one framework treats the self as broken, the other treats it as incomplete.
