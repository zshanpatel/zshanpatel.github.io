> The repeated dismantling of the self's false priorities through surrender before God.

The ego does not surrender once. It rebuilds, reorganises, and reasserts its hierarchy of desires between every prayer. Modern self-help treats transformation as a single epiphany or a visible milestone. Islamic spiritual discipline treats it as a daily, hourly, structural interruption.

In the Islamic tradition, *tazkiyah* (tazkiyah) is the systematic purification of the nafs (nafs) through worship, reflection, and obedience. Classical scholars like al-Ghazali and Ibn al-Qayyim described three stages of the self: nafs al-ammara (the commanding self, demanding sin), nafs al-lawwama (the self-reproaching soul, aware of its faults), and nafs al-mutma'innah (the tranquil soul, at rest in divine alignment). The movement between these stages is not automatic. It requires a practice designed to interrupt the ego at the very moment it feels most justified.

Prayer five times daily, on time, functions as that interruption. At the peak of a competing desire, at the height of a plan or a pursuit, the practitioner stops. The mind computes, compares, and contends against its own priorities. The question is not abstract: what matters most right now? The act of bowing, of placing the forehead on the ground, is the physical answer. The hierarchy is re-established. The ego is purified, again and again, not through a single act of will but through an architecture of repeated surrender.
