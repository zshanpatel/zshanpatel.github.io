> Historical rhyme is the recurrence of structural patterns across different periods, where analogous conditions produce analogous outcomes without exact repetition.

The phrase "history rhymes" captures a structural observation: certain patterns recur because the underlying mechanisms that produce them remain constant. The cycle of group solidarity rising through hardship, achieving success, dissolving through comfort, and being overthrown by a hungrier group from the periphery is not specific to any culture, era, or location. It is a structural dynamic that repeats whenever the conditions that produce collective cohesion are replaced by conditions that dissolve it.

Historical rhyme differs from historical repetition. Repetition implies identical recurrence. Rhyme implies analogous recurrence: the same structural dynamics operating in different contexts, producing similar but not identical outcomes. The specific actors, ideologies, and circumstances change. The underlying mechanism remains constant.

The recognition of historical rhyme is itself a form of knowledge. It allows diagnosis of present conditions by reference to structural parallels in the past. It also provides warning: if the pattern is structural rather than contingent, then the outcomes it produced before are likely to recur unless the structural conditions change.
