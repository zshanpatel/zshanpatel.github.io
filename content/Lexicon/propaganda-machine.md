> Propaganda becomes a machine when influence is no longer an isolated message but an organised system that keeps producing the conditions for belief.

The phrase propaganda machine is often used as if propaganda were only a powerful piece of content. The more important object is the apparatus behind it: people who define the objective, channels that distribute the message, data that identifies audiences, and feedback systems that measure what moves them. The machine turns persuasion into a repeatable institutional process.

A propaganda machine can use states, political movements, media organisations, advertisers, platforms, or networks of apparently independent accounts. Its messages may be openly attributed, disguised, or mixed across different voices. What unifies the system is not one ideology or one medium, but the deliberate coordination of symbols, narratives, timing, repetition, and targeting toward a specified collective outcome.

Digital networks have not made propaganda obsolete. They have changed its operating environment. A system can now segment audiences at fine resolution, test messages rapidly, and make coordinated influence appear to be spontaneous public opinion. The machine is therefore best understood as an infrastructure of attention and interpretation, not merely as a catalogue of lies.

*Source: Bastos, M.T. & Farkas, J. (2019). "Donald Trump is my President!" The Internet Research Agency Propaganda Machine. Social Media + Society, 5(3); Smith, B.L. (2026). "Propaganda." Encyclopaedia Britannica.*
