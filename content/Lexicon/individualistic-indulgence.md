> The self that worships itself will always find a justification for excess.

Individualistic indulgence is the pattern of treating personal gratification as a right rather than a tendency. It is not merely indulgence. It is indulgence with a philosophical framework that makes refusal read as oppression. The person who overeats is not overeating. They are practising self-love. The person who abandons responsibility is not abandoning responsibility. They are prioritising their mental health. The person who celebrates themselves endlessly is not narcissistic. They are authentic.

The pattern is structurally identical to *[[ego-worship]]*, but with a specific cultural packaging. Where ego-worship is the raw psychological mechanism, individualistic indulgence is its socially approved expression. It is the mechanism dressed in the language of rights, identity, and personal truth. It is the most dangerous form of self-deception because it disguises itself as self-awareness.

The cultural function of individualistic indulgence is to prevent collective action. A population of individuals who each believe their own gratification is the highest moral good will never organise. They will never sacrifice. They will never build anything larger than themselves. They will consume, perform, and validate each other's consumption and performance, in an endless cycle that benefits the economic system and no one else.

The Islamic counter-framing is not austerity for its own sake. It is *[[divine-purpose]]*: the redirection of desire toward something that does not collapse under its own weight. The self is not suppressed. It is oriented. The distinction is the entire difference between a life of accumulation and a life of meaning.
