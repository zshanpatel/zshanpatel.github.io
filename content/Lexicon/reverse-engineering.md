> Reverse engineering is the disciplined act of moving backward from an observed result to the system that made it possible.

Most people meet a successful product, advertisement, or system only at its surface. They see the finished object and either admire it or imitate its visible features. That misses the real source of its power: the sequence of decisions underneath it.

Reverse engineering starts with the outcome and works backward. It asks what audience was being addressed, what problem or desire was identified, what position was chosen, what message was constructed, and what channel or mechanism carried it. The goal is not to reproduce the artifact. It is to expose the logic that made the artifact effective, so that the logic can be tested rather than blindly copied.

In marketing, this makes an advertisement readable as a designed intervention. Its pace, category, emotional cues, target audience, and promised transformation become evidence of a strategy. The analysis remains an interpretation of what is publicly visible, not access to the creator's private intentions.

*Source: Eran Livneh, "Reverse-Engineer Your Marketing," MarketingProfs. Practitioner source defining reverse engineering as working back from an end result to a solution.*
