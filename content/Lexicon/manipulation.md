---
title: Manipulation
name: manipulation
description: Influence that works by bypassing a target's rational agency — through deception, emotional exploitation, or engineered choice architecture — rather than by offering reasons a person could evaluate and accept or reject on the merits.
tags:

> Persuasion gives you a reason. Manipulation gives you a push you don't get to see coming.

The line separating legitimate persuasion from manipulation isn't the presence of emotion or the absence of facts — it's whether the influence works *through* a target's rational capacities or *around* them. An honest argument, even a passionate one, still asks something of the listener's judgment. Manipulation is designed specifically to route around that judgment: through deception, exploiting a known bias, or engineering the situation so the "choice" was never really open.

Contemporary philosophical and legal scholarship on influence treats this as a genuinely difficult line to draw in practice — plenty of ordinary marketing and political messaging sits in a grey zone between the two — but the underlying test (does this respect or bypass the audience's own reasoning) is what the field keeps returning to.

*Source: Sunstein, C.R. (2016). "Fifty Shades of Manipulation." In The Ethics of Influence: Government in the Age of Behavioral Science. Cambridge University Press.*
