> Hope-seeking keeps the possibility of a better future emotionally alive without requiring the action that would make it more likely.

Hope can orient a person toward meaningful action. Hope-seeking is different. It is the repeated consumption of promises, predictions, motivational signals, and imagined futures as a substitute for choosing a goal and acting under constraint. The person feels near to progress because anticipation itself supplies emotional movement.

Advertising and media can exploit this pattern by keeping arrival permanently ahead. Each new video, product, or forecast offers another reason to wait for transformation. The audience remains engaged with the possibility of becoming different while the conditions producing inaction remain untouched.

The distinction is therefore practical rather than cynical. Grounded hope clarifies an end and strengthens action. Hope-seeking protects the feeling of possibility while postponing responsibility for the next concrete step.

Source: Snyder, *The Psychology of Hope*; Oettingen, *Rethinking Positive Thinking*.
