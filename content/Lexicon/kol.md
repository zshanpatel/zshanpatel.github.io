> Followed for their judgment, not their reach — which is exactly what makes their endorsement worth paying for.

A KOL (Key Opinion Leader) is a credible, established voice on a specific crypto topic — a chain, a sector, a trading style — whose audience trusts their analysis rather than following them purely for entertainment. That trust is what separates a KOL from a generic influencer: reach measures how many people see a post, credibility measures how many people act on it.

Projects pay KOLs to promote tokens precisely because that trust converts into buying activity. The incentive problem is obvious: a KOL's commentary can be genuine analysis or paid promotion wearing the same voice, and retail traders rarely know which they're reading.

The mechanism itself isn't new — "KOL" is crypto's rebrand of "opinion leader," a concept from 1940s mass-communication research: most people don't take their views straight from the media, they take them from a trusted person in their own network who already interpreted it. Crypto just moved that trusted person from the neighbourhood to Twitter.

*Source: Katz, E., & Lazarsfeld, P. F. (1955), Personal Influence: The Part Played by People in the Flow of Mass Communications.*
