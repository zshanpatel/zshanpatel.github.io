> A birthday is the annual celebration of the self on a day with no divine significance, absorbed from the very systems that eroded the foundation it claims to stand on.

The practice feels so universal, so harmless, that questioning it sounds absurd. Of course you celebrate a birthday. Everyone does. It is tradition. It is joy. It is family. But tradition is not self-justifying, and the question worth asking is not whether the practice is harmful in any obvious, immediate sense, but what it trains a person to do with their attention, their gratitude, and their sense of where value comes from.

A birthday celebration centres the self. The rituals are unmistakable: gifts directed at the individual, attention directed at the individual,food and decoration oriented around commemorating the individual's arrival. The theological question is not whether selfhood matters, but whether the self is the highest thing worth celebrating. In a framework oriented toward the divine, the answer is no. The self is a trust, not an achievement. Its value derives from its relationship to the Creator, not from its own existence as a milestone. A birthday, in this reading, is the annual reinforcement of a secular axiom: that the individual is the unit of significance, and that the appropriate response to having existed for another year is self-celebration.

The deeper problem is the origin of the practice. Birthday celebration as a cultural norm is absorbed from secular, Westernised systems, not derived from any Islamic textual source. This is not a historical accident. It is a pattern: practices that centre the self are absorbed seamlessly into communities that have already begun eroding their spiritual foundations, because those communities are primed to accept anything that feels normal and requires no discipline. The practice arrives pre-normalised. It does not need to be defended because no one is attacking it. And that is precisely the mechanism of systemic violence. It does not announce itself. It moves in as tradition, and by the time anyone thought to question it, questioning would feel like the aggressive act.
