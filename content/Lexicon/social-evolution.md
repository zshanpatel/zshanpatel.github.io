> The belief that society evolves naturally is the most useful fiction power ever invented.

Social evolution is the assumption that cultural change is organic, directional, and inherently improving. That societies move from primitive to advanced, from oppressive to liberal, from traditional to modern, and that this movement is as natural as biological evolution. The assumption is so deeply embedded in secular thought that questioning it reads as reactionary.

The reality is that most significant cultural shifts are engineered. The sexual revolution was funded. Feminism was institutionalised. Consumerism was planned. The shift from communal to individualistic social structures did not happen because people spontaneously decided independence was better than belonging. It happened because economic systems required mobile labour, fragmented families, and consumers who would fill the void with purchases.

The framework of social evolution serves a specific function: it makes resistance to cultural change read as resistance to nature itself. If progress is natural, then opposing it is unnatural. This is the same logic that pathologises [[masculine-leadership]] as "toxic" and frames traditional gender roles as "backwards." The evolution metaphor does the ideological work of making engineered change feel inevitable.

In reality, civilisations do not evolve. They are shaped. The question is never whether change is happening, but who is directing it and toward what end. The [[neo-colonial-project]] does not announce itself as conquest. It presents itself as progress.
