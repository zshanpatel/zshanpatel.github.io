> Four different subjects, one shared claim: that the world's real structure is written in number.

Where the trivium trained a student in language and argument, the quadrivium — the "four ways" — trained the mind in abstraction: arithmetic (number in itself), geometry (number in space), astronomy (number in motion), and music (number in time and sound). The organising idea, formalised above all by Boethius in the 6th century, was that these four weren't separate hobbies but one connected discipline in numerical relationship, building toward the highest object of knowledge.

Historians of science treat the quadrivium as a genuinely stable, centuries-long scheme for organising knowledge — one that didn't just precede modern science but gradually broke apart into it, as each of its four disciplines took on an increasingly practical, empirical life of its own between the 13th and 17th centuries.

*Source: Valleriani, M. (2022). "From the Quadrivium to Modern Science." History of Science and Technology.*
