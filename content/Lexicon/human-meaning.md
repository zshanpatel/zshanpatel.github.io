> Meaning isn't a stronger flavor of happiness. It's a different measurement, and the two can point in opposite directions at once.

Viktor Frankl's logotherapy started from a clinical observation: people can endure severe suffering if they believe it connects to something meaningful, and people with every material comfort can still report their lives as empty. Meaning, in this framework, isn't a byproduct of pleasure — it's a separate axis entirely, built from purpose, coherence, and significance rather than from moment-to-moment positive feeling.

The empirical research since has held up the distinction: meaning in life correlates strongly with psychological well-being, but through a different mechanism than positive affect does, and people routinely choose meaningful-but-difficult paths over easy-but-empty ones. That's the gap this essay points to when it says AI can execute tasks but can't tell whether a task is worth doing — execution optimizes for output; meaning asks a question output can't answer on its own.

Source: Zika, S. & Chamberlain, K. "On the relation between meaning in life and psychological well-being," *British Journal of Psychology*, 1992; Frankl, V. *Man's Search for Meaning*, 1946/1959.
