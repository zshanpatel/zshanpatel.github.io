> Historical repetition is the recurrence of structural dynamics across periods, where systems of power concentrate, decay, and are overthrown in predictable cycles.

Historical repetition differs from historical rhyme. Rhyme implies analogous recurrence: similar dynamics in different contexts. Repetition implies the same dynamics operating in the same way across different periods. The specific actors change. The underlying mechanism remains constant.

The essay identifies a specific pattern of historical repetition: the cycle of group solidarity rising through hardship, achieving success, dissolving through comfort, and being overthrown by a hungrier group from the periphery. This pattern repeats because the underlying mechanism is structural, not cultural. The groups change. The ideology changes. The geography changes. The mechanism remains the same.

The recognition of historical repetition is itself a form of knowledge. It allows diagnosis of present conditions by reference to structural parallels in the past. It also provides warning: if the pattern is structural rather than contingent, then the outcomes it produced before are likely to recur unless the structural conditions change.
