> Social balance is the actual distribution of power, wealth, and opportunity across groups, distinct from the ideological claim that existing distributions reflect natural merit or deserve their current form.

The phrase "social balance" names the outcome of historical processes: colonisation, economic hierarchy, systemic oppression, and the distribution of access to capital, education, and institutional power. When this balance is described as neutral or natural, it obscures the mechanisms that produced it.

The claim that social balance is "not in our favour" is not a claim about victimhood. It is a structural observation about where power, resources, and opportunity concentrate. The observation is diagnostic: understanding the balance as produced by systems rather than by nature is the prerequisite for changing it.

Social balance is maintained by the same structures that created it. Economic hierarchy reproduces wealth concentration. Educational systems reproduce knowledge hierarchies. Political systems reproduce decision-making power. The balance is not static but actively maintained through the reproduction of the conditions that produced it.
