> Not "learning to code" — the study of what can be computed at all, and how efficiently, independent of which language happens to be fashionable this decade.

The ACM and IEEE jointly define what an undergraduate Computer Science degree actually has to cover — theory of computation, algorithms, systems, and increasingly data science and security — in a curriculum guideline revised roughly every decade (most recently *Computing Curricula 2020*). That's worth separating from the popular conflation of "Computer Science degree" with "learned to write code": coding is one applied skill that sits inside the discipline, not a synonym for it, and it's that applied, executional layer AI automation is currently compressing, not necessarily the theoretical core underneath it.
