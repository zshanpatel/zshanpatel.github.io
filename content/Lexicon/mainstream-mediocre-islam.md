> The "moderate" Muslim is not a middle ground. They are the product of a system that neutered their deen and called it balance.

Islamic tradition does not recognise a "moderate" Islam. It recognises Islam. The deen is a complete system of belief, law, economics, and governance, not a private spiritual hobby to be practised in isolation from public life. The concept of deen itself, rooted in obligation, judgment, and submission, describes an integrated framework that leaves no compartment of human existence outside divine authority.

Mainstream mediocre Islam is what emerges when that integration is deliberately removed. It is Islam stripped of its political edge, its economic critique, its collective accountability, and its insistence on divine sovereignty over human legislation. What remains is a set of private rituals and vague moral sentiments, conveniently compatible with secular liberal order. It does not challenge interest-based banking. It does not question man-made legal systems. It does not speak about heaven and hell as structural realities that shape present action. It asks nothing of its adherents that the surrounding culture would find uncomfortable.

This is not Islam at its centre. It is Islam at its most tamed. The label "extremist" is always measured against this hollowed-out baseline. The more neutered the baseline becomes, the more any authentic practice appears extreme by comparison. The system does not need to silence Islam outright. It only needs to redefine "acceptable Islam" as something that never challenges anything. Those who fall within that redefined boundary are praised as moderate. Those who do not are labelled extremists. The game is rigged: the baseline was designed to be below the standard, so that meeting the standard automatically qualifies as going too far.
