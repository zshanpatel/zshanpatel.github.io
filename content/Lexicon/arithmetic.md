> Arithmetic is the oldest, most foundational branch of mathematics. It is the modern equivalent of what we call Number Theory.
> 
> In the modern era, arithmetic is strictly associated with concrete numbers and calculations using the basic operations (addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division).

In ancient Greece (where the word _arithmetike_ originated, meaning "the art of counting"), arithmetic was not viewed as a single subject.

Ancient scholars like Pythagoras looked at numbers as abstract concepts (e.g., studying even/odd numbers, prime numbers, or perfect numbers). They considered this "true" science. This branch was known as Arithmetica.

Then there was Logistica. The actual art of calculation and computing. This involved the daily mechanical tasks of using addition, subtraction, and multiplication tables to solve commercial business, trade, and tax problems. The elite ancient Greeks actually looked down on this branch, leaving routine computations to calculators or slaves.

Before you study shapes, motion, or sound, you study the relationships that all three are secretly made of.

In the classical [[quadrivium]], arithmetic came first because it was considered the simplest and most fundamental of the four mathematical arts: number in itself, with no reference to space, motion, or sound. Boethius's 6th-century treatise on the subject, the standard textbook for centuries of European education, followed a Neopythagorean tradition treating numerical ratios as carrying ethical and even cosmological significance. The same proportions that governed harmony and celestial motion, the theory went, also governed the well-ordered soul.

That framing is why arithmetic sat inside a liberal art rather than a trade: it wasn't taught as a practical counting skill but as the discipline that trained the mind to grasp abstract relationships before applying that same capacity to geometry, astronomy, and music.

*Source: Panti, C. (2018). "Beauty, Ethics and Numbers in Boethius' Quadrivial Treatises." Aisthesis, 11(1).*
