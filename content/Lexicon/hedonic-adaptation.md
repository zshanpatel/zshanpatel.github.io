> The automatic reduction in emotional intensity from any circumstance, good or bad, that resets your experience to a baseline you cannot feel moving.

Hedonic adaptation is the well-documented tendency for the emotional impact of any significant change, whether a gain or a loss, to fade over time until a person returns to a stable baseline level of subjective well-being. The concept was formally described by Brickman and Campbell in 1971 and refined by Frederick and Loewenstein in 1999, who classified three distinct mechanisms: shifting adaptation levels, where the definition of "normal" moves to accommodate the new condition; desensitization, where the nervous system literally registers less of the stimulus; and sensitization, where certain stimuli gain power even as others fade. The result is that the human mind does not track cumulative change well. It registers the first hit. It barely notices the hundredth.

This is the mechanism that makes slow, incremental erosion so difficult to detect from the inside. A person adapting to each small loss, each fraction of capacity surrendered, never registers the cumulative trajectory because each individual step is too small to trigger alarm. The baseline has simply moved. What was once unacceptable now feels normal, and what once felt normal is no longer remembered. This is distinct from the hedonic treadmill, which is the treadmill metaphor itself. Hedonic adaptation is the mechanism that keeps the treadmill running.

The dark side of hedonic adaptation is precisely this: it can adapt you to a degraded state as smoothly as it adapts you to an elevated one. Ahmed and Koob's 1998 research in *Science* demonstrated that drug use in rats could actually shift the hedonic set point itself downward, meaning the baseline was not merely tolerated but permanently lowered. The person inside the adaptation does not experience a fall. They experience a series of moments that each feel ordinary, each one indistinguishable from the last, the cumulative effect invisible precisely because the detection mechanism has been neutralized.
