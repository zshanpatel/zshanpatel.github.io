> Job portals are the digital gatekeepers of modern employment, algorithmically sorting applicants into hireable and invisible.

The modern job search runs through platforms that aggregate listings, filter applicants, and mediate the relationship between employer and candidate. What was once a direct conversation become a transaction mediated by algorithms, keywords, and ATS systems designed to reduce human beings to data points.

Job portals concentrate power on the employer side. They set the terms, define the filters, and decide who gets seen. Applicants must optimize for machine readability rather than genuine qualification, tailoring resumes to algorithmic preferences rather than human judgment. The result is a system where the most qualified candidates can be invisible while those who game the system rise to the top.

The portal model transforms employment from a relationship into a market, where labour is a commodity and workers are interchangeable units of production. This commodification serves capital by suppressing wages and increasing employer leverage, while creating the illusion of opportunity through the sheer volume of listings.
