> The greatest of God’s saints remain hidden

\- Shams of Tabriz

There is a love that I have felt, an unconditional one. It can’t be captured in words. I have forever roamed the corners of the world to find a hint of that love. I remain a wanderer, my expectations dying as each day passes by. My only wish, if I had cherished it when I had the chance. But alas, we only really feel once we have lost and never truly understand what we already have. Perhaps I continue making that mistake.

The first born is always loved the most, but a bond like this is probably formed once in a few hundred years. There is a natural telepathic connection between a father and son. An unspoken one. The son is an image born of the father. This time the father aligned the heavens and hell for a son. It was so strong that it would drive me numb. I am that son, whose heart only opened after I lost him. It was all too quick. In a split second I lost the light that forever showed me a path that I so blatantly ignored. And now he was gone. And left me with his words.

He was the silent types really. He would only speak when spoken to. A man of few words, but when he did speak, they would cut through you like a hot knife cuts through butter. Most of the times, he had a simple smile on his face, one I always doubted. How could a man be so patient in a world full of darkness? What madness drove him to keep a happy face, I wouldn’t know this till it was too late. It was me. I was the legacy he thought he was going to leave behind. 

An ambitious man, in all the wrong places I thought. Money and fame meant very little to him. If he had his way, he would do away with it all. Happiness and love was his ammunition. He never wanted more than he needed. He accumulated worldly possessions for his family. If I had to truly think of what he really wanted, it was just peace. Simple peace. Many mistook him for a religious man. He was far more than religion. He deeply cared for kindness and humility.

A revolution brew inside him, he and I lived through the digital transition of the 90s. He had seen the worlds change, and change it did for him. From the corners of a small town to the hustle of the city in a distant land. He had come a long way and back. He carried with him the burdens of this dark world he had come from, an underworld he had experienced but chose to show us very little of. Perhaps he thought he was protecting us. 

From the genocide of Bosnia to the war crimes in Afghanistan, he had kept an account of it all through his lifetime. We felt the pain of every soul that suffered, dead or alive. We would talk about the persecution of Muslims in history and he would let an occasional sigh slip through, I was one of his confidants besides my mother. I don’t know if he saw the effects it had on me, or perhaps he did and thought it would do well for me to know this side of reality, one we were kept far from. An unpleasant one. He held it close to his heart. He wanted change, a revolution. One he died with. It killed us both a little inside each time we heard it through the mouths of a warmongering news anchor, it was not his fault I thought; he was getting paid for it. But my father never saw it that way, worldly riches meant very little to him. He wondered, how a human being can allow another’s suffering, a thought he struggled with. His heart spoke a language that was soft and humble. There was only one reason for a man’s action;

Love.
