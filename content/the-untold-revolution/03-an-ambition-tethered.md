April 22, 1980

India was now a free country. What the British left was up for grabs. And the countrymen did grab it. 

Only this time, they missed out one crucial factor; the rights of humans. 

Men and women were by the numbers and there was no lawfulness, the British as ruthless as they had been portrayed still valued life. But the new India only valued one thing; money.

Religion played a huge part in shaping the new countryof India. Superstitions, although hampered over the years still governed the thinking. Gods and goddesses were by the number. 

India, a headless chicken, now the country found its purpose in the pursuit of making money for themselves. It didn’t matter what it took. Morals and empathy was for dinner conversations. The outside world was tough and here nothing else mattered.

Every workplace had an underbelly. Bribery and commissions was the norm of the country. And human life was so easy and convenient to waste if somebody didn’t comply.

Ishaque set out of his small town to the deep jungle, a village called Parbhani. He had just completed his diploma in Civil Engineering and had been posted to a new job fresh out of college. With the desire to do something great, the world was up for grabs he thought. He couldn’t have been more wrong.

“Here Ishaque, this is your cut” said his manager shoving a package that looked like it had two stacks of money.  

The manager had a large grin on his face.

“What is this for?” Ishaque inquired.

“Keep it and make sure you report twenty tons of cement instead of five”

“But we have used only four point five tons” he got back his voice still shaky, he did not fully understand what he was being asked to do.

The manager raising his voice slowly “Do what you have been told to do and you will have a bright future”

Suddenly realizing his throat was dry, Ishaque took a gulp and said “What if I don’t want the money?” The smile on his manager’s face that stretched from ear to ear slowly started to become smaller by each word.

And just as quickly, his manager snaps back at him “Then you will be buried here in this five ton cement”

“Four point eight” he thought, not daring to bring his thoughts to his lips. He understood he had a sword hanging on his head, his time here was limited and it was now clear to him. He had to leave that place. Ungodly he thought, he wanted to leave all this behind.

So he quit and left in search of greener pastures.
