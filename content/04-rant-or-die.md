Through my lens: the perfect man in an imperfect world. 

It feels like, losing him took so much of my strength out of me. I fought to put myself back together. Most days I feel like I will burst with rage. But when I look at my mother’s upset face, everything fades and I stop thinking about the anger. My sanity comes back to me and I try to make her smile, in the next chance I get. Wouldn’t want to make it too obvious that I am just  a frustrated little man-boy on the inside who isn’t half of what his father was. 

After it all began, I broke; from myself and free from the world. I took my time to figure out the difference between the pieces of me that actually mattered versus those that I carried unnecessarily. Or apprehend if anything actually mattered. I have now come to understand, the hardest thing to do is realizing what’s really important, before putting in the effort. Doing something is fairly easy, comprehending the thought that goes into that action is the hard part. I now spend most of my time reflecting his acts against mine.

Going back, it was when I was in fifth grade, I think he realized there was more to life than coming home after a hard day’s work. He grew up believing what he was taught in the place he grew up in; work was worship and everything else was noise. He couldn’t sit idle without doing something to keep himself busy. I didn’t disagree completely and the same mindset was somehow ingrained in us. 

During this time, I feel I witnessed him dissecting his emotions and taking control of them. Being away from his birthplace, gave his mind the freedom to be free and think about what was actually important, besides apportioned food and timely sleep. I watched him go against the hardwired customs his childhood had entrenched in him. But I didn’t grow up in the same world.

Fast forward to my grown-up time with him; to me one of his bigger achievements was being steadfast through whatever life put him through, even when every particle of the universe was going against him, he stood his ground. His priorities, as I understood were divided in two parts; his religious beliefs and the world. As much as he wanted to get away from the latter, it chased him till the end. 

I never completely understood what he was, till he was gone. 

For instance, on hearing the call to prayer when he rushed towards God, I would run in the opposite direction. It was impossible to live up to his beliefs and standards, so I wanted to defy it all. I was afraid I couldn't be as great as he was. Here I was, just an ordinary punk, and that’s all I wanted to be. The obvious choice seemed, to not accept the path he was leading me on. Religion was for the faint-hearted I thought. And I had already gotten more academic recognitions than he did, so all the wisdom was just extra. I was a rebel just like he had been, only I was for all the wrong reasons. 

Ten years later, his tolerance, a hug and a hard line whispered in my ear by him, got me thinking: I had not understood his impartiality to this world and I was still trying to prove to him that I was better. I had always felt I never fulfilled his wish to see me become the best person I could. I believe he knew I was trying but he couldn't forgo his habit to push me harder and I couldn't beat mine to misunderstand his intentions. 

Consequently, my anarchic-rebellion had run its course. I now longed to make him proud. And I would go to any lengths to do just that. Towards the end, I realized he smiled through most of what I had been putting him through or sometimes cried behind my back, as I was to find out later after he left me. I arrive on point but I am always late. He understood that. 

In actuality, he could be described as an undermined perfect storm. He didn't want to command or conquer anyone. He wanted to help everyone if he could. Brute force wasn't his weapon of choice. Patience was his greatest virtue. He didn't want to fight, he believed love could deliver us all. He didn't want power, knowledge was his capital. He didn't want to deal in anger and hate, he wanted to spread kindness and compassion. He didn't want the riches of the world, only enough to spread it around him. His struggle was with distrust and greed. Happiness and affection was his pursuit. He understood revenge could not liberate us, but only forgiveness could set us free; firstly forgiving the self for the wrong we have done to ourselves and then forgiving the ones around for the wrong they have done to us. He didn’t hate, he had seen the unloved carry hate in to the abyss.

Make no mistake, we are in that pit right now. The light seems too far to reach. But we can choose to either rise from this fate together as a team or perish in the viciousness of repressors. 

Somewhere, we have lost his way. Anger has poisoned our souls. We have learned to react and not act. We have stopped chasing his truth and have settled for the hate of tyrants. We deviate towards the common road more taken, to steer clear of looking inside ourselves. By blaming the world we have imprisoned ourselves in our despair.  

Witness this, we will prevail. His memory will run in our veins and torment us till the absolute end but we will be victorious. The power they took from us will return to the righteous. The hate of the oppressors will pass, but his sanctity will remain. I have felt his unbiased love, we will have his legacy too.

I stand here, trying to hold my ground, ready to break any moment. Not sure if I will be able to put myself together the next time. Only thing I know for sure is I lose a few parts every time I piece together myself. I pray for the strength to hold on to his love, to hold on to my family even if I have to lose everything else.

- Feeble son of a man who carried mountains with a smile on his face
